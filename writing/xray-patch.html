Good idea, wrong home: a post-mortem of my KOReader patch

Duccio Mondanelli · 2026 · ~9 min read

I wanted one small thing in KOReader. When xray.koplugin (a very cool plugin developed by ultimatejimmy) finds people and places, show me who they are inline, without leaving the page. Tap to reveal, underlined in text.

A couple evenings later I had it. Entity Footnotes. The more interesting part is what happened next. The maintainer said yes to the idea and no to the location. That no reshaped the whole thing.

This is the post-mortem on that patch, on the "no" that reshaped it, and on why solving it right meant not touching the plugin at all.

The problem: focused reading, not distracted tapping

The naive fix would scan the whole book once and list all entities. That breaks the reading process with an annoying popup, a freeze while it computes, and another reason to stop reading early. X-Ray discovers as it goes.

The principle

The real shape of a problem only shows up once you look at how the data actually arrives, not how you assumed it would.

The build

1. Do not fork it: patch it at runtime

My first instinct was also wrong. Fork the plugin. That would mean maintaining a fork for a small feature. The no from the maintainer forced me to think of a better angle. Patch at runtime instead. Lua allows it. The patch rides on top, reuses the tap interaction the plugin already had, and lives separately. The maintainer keeps ownership. I keep my patch.

2. Scan in chunks, in backgrund

E-ink forced the next decision. Full book scans hang the device. My first version did that. Terrible. So it scans in chunks, in the backgrund, while you keep reading. You can add features forever. They need to work well, though.

The principle

The right system boundary isn't only about the features; it's about scalability and the end-user experience. You can always add features, but you can't always fix a bad experience.

Things kept breaking

Bugs made the tradeoffs concrete. Whole book hangs. Rescan loops on books with zero hits. Raw locale keys in UI. I fixed what mattered and chose my failure mode on purpose elsewhere, like case sensitive matching to cut false positives even if ALL CAPS gets missed. Stable and usable beat perfect. There is always time to polish after.

The principle

Sometimes you can't fix all the problems, but you can choose your failure mode on purpose.

Taking "no" as data, not a rejection

Taking no as data helped most. The objection was specific. Repeated rescans as a guaranteed UX across every e-ink device. That deserves respect. It was about real use, not ego. So I did not push. I built around the boundary, met the project where it was, reused its patterns, left its shape intact.

The principle

Sometimes, a "no" is a precise description of a boundary, and the collaborative move is finding the shape that fits it, not arguing for your solution.

Where this connects to my day job

The same lesson shows at work. Do not design for an ideal candidate with a perfect week. Look at schedules, energy, support access. Then shape help to that reality. If it only works in the abstract, it fails the people it claims to serve.

The principle

Good design starts by respecting the situation a person is actually living, not the idealized version we wish they had.

What I'd take from this back to work

Start with real constraints. Fit conditions as lived, not as wished. Build with users and leave room for no. Simple and practical usually wins because it survives real life.

Code is on GitHub, discussion on xray.koplugin#100, if you want to compare notes. Find me on LinkedIn if you want to compare notes or swap ideas.

Footnote: I guess my idea was good, because xray eventually pulled the feature in their core app!
