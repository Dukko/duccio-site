Teaching an app to read a receipt

Duccio Mondanelli · 2026 · ~5 min read

I use YNAB and I hate typing a Costco run line by line almost as much as I love the budget. One receipt can be groceries, house goods, and dog food. I want three splits, not one dump.

So I built a small self hosted PWA. Point the phone, AI reads the lines, it creates the split. This is the post mortem, and why the hard parts were surprisingly similar to what I do in my day job.

The problem: a generic reader is not enough

The app guesses. "Costco" becomes "Groceries." Done. That works until it hits my real budget. I do not use default categories. I use ones I renamed over years to match how I think. A generic reader mapping to generic names just moves cleanup later. It does not remove it.

Same failure as a generic form for two licenses. Plausible, not specifically correct.

The principle

A tool that outputs a plausible answer instead of the specifically correct one just moves the cleanup work later, it doesn't remove it.

The build

The app was the result of three choices.

1. Capture cheaply

PWA, no app store. Photo or gallery pick, compressed on device. Usable in a parking lot.

2. Give it my real categories

The app fetches my live YNAB list first and sends it with the photo. Claude or Gemini does not invent names. It matches lines to names that exist. Same move as the Hardcover app: do not ask it to reason in the abstract. Give it the structure for this one setup.

3. Nothing posts until I say so

Output lands on a confirm screen. I fix, then it creates the transaction or subtransactions. The model does the tedious pass. It does not touch the record unsupervised.

The principle

AI output becomes a record only after a human who knows the data confirms it. Convenience is the AI's job, trust is still mine.

Where this connects to NXT GEN

That rule mirrors our dashboard rework. Two years of sheets drifted because hand summaries no longer matched source data. A generic parser would drift from my budget the same way. The fix is the same. Ground it in the current source of truth, then require a human check. At work no number leaves until someone who knows the candidates signs off. At home no line becomes a transaction until I look.

The principle

Whether the record is a grant report or a grocery split, AI is only as trustworthy as the specific data it's grounded in and the verification step before it counts as real.

What I'd take from this back to work

Feed the model the real structure. Keep output one step from the record. Build the boring automation anyway. It saves minutes a week and probably your sanity.

If you are building small tools to automate the parts of your own life you actually hate, I'd enjoy comparing notes. Find me on LinkedIn, or see the code on GitHub.

Footnote: I moved to ZeroSum, so this app is retired. I am leaving the post as is. Not everything needs to outlive what it was built for.
