From Hornbooks to LLMs

Duccio Mondanelli · 2026 · ~2 min read

When we think of Ed Tech, this is probably not the first thing that comes to mind.

The hornbook was one of the earliest educational tools used to teach children to read: a paddle-shaped item, often carved, holding a piece of (expensive) vellum and covered with horn (hence the name). The hornbook qualifies in my mind as one of the first Ed Tech tools because of its purpose (teaching) and its intention (improving teaching via tools), which are key characteristics of Educational Technology.

We have made a lot of progress since then. Today, you can design a customized and interactive tutor for your students through AI and prompt engineering, so that your 11th graders can practice their US History skills at their own pace and level. Or you can damage your students' cognitive development. Like all Ed Tech tools, AI can be used in a conscientious and responsible way, or it can be recklessly misused and inflict irreparable harm on our students. (I can picture other ways a hornbook could have been used in the 1700s, especially to teach discipline or manners.)

Ultimately, the history, or histories, of Ed Tech show us that education will continue to explore ways to make learning more accessible. Just like the humble hornbook was a way to increase access to education by allowing children to learn wherever they were, modern Ed Tech tools can create new opportunities for learning and teaching. It is up to us to choose how, when, and whether we use the tools we have at our fingertips.
